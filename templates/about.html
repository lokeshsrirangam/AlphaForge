About AlphaForge

AlphaForge is a backtesting tool for a moving-average trend strategy. It is built to answer one question honestly: would this rule have made money after costs, and is the result distinguishable from luck?

How a backtest runs

  1. Load adjusted daily closes from Yahoo Finance (cached locally).
  2. Run either a fast/slow moving-average crossover or a Donchian breakout with separate breakout-entry and channel-exit windows.
  3. Size the position with volatility targeting, so exposure shrinks when the market gets wild.
  4. Execute on the next close, charge trading costs on every change in position, and apply an optional close-based stop.
  5. Report performance net of costs next to buy & hold, measured over the same window.

Design decisions

  • No look-ahead. A signal computed on day t is traded on day t+1. A test checks that changing future prices cannot change past positions.
  • Costs are on by default. Brokerage, taxes and slippage are modelled as basis points on the amount traded.
  • Fair benchmark. Strategy and buy & hold are compared from the first bar the strategy could have traded.
  • Statistical honesty. Sharpe is shown with its standard error, and the dashboard warns about too few trades, short samples and suspiciously high results.
  • Null test. On driftless random walks the average Sharpe is about zero, which shows the engine does not manufacture edge.

Research diagnostics

  • Return calendar: visualizes monthly compounded strategy returns to expose uneven performance through time.
  • Regime attribution: compares strategy and benchmark daily returns when the prior close is above or below its 200-session average.
  • Walk-forward evaluation: tests parameter selection on later data that was not used to choose those parameters.
  • Two signal families: moving-average trend following and a close-based Donchian breakout, both evaluated with the same sizing, execution and cost model.

These views describe the historical sample. Regime slices are conditional and non-contiguous; parameter searches and backtests can still overfit.

Known limitations

  • One instrument at a time, long-only, daily bars.
  • Stops are checked on closing prices only; intraday gaps through the stop are not seen.
  • Free Yahoo data can contain errors and survivorship bias in single stocks.
  • A good backtest is not a forecast. Nothing here is investment advice.

Technology

Python, pandas and NumPy for the engine; Flask for the web app; Plotly for charts; Bootstrap for layout; yfinance for market data; pytest for tests.
